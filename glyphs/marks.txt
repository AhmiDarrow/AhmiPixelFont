# Ahmi Pixel — accent marks and the shortened capitals they sit on
#
# Marks are drawn once here and placed by build.py (see the top of
# ahmi-pixel.txt). Over lowercase letters, two-row marks fill the 8th row
# of the em and the cap-line row, with the empty row between mark and
# letter that the i already has. Over capitals the one-row marks (dot,
# diaeresis) leave the capital 6 rows tall and two-row marks
# leave it 5 rows tall, the way 8-bit system fonts fitted accents into an
# 8-pixel cell. A mark named "<mark>.cap" would be used over capitals
# instead; none is needed yet.
#
# Each mark is drawn in a box as wide as the letters it sits on: 5 columns
# for odd-width bases, 6 for even ones (build.py centres the box on the
# base). The shapes were chosen by trying the candidates against every pair
# of marks that share a letter (e/é/è/ê/ë/ē/ĕ/ė/ę/ě ...), so that any two
# differ by at least 3 pixels in Regular and in Bold, where neighbouring
# pixels merge: acute leans over the right half and grave over the left,
# the dot is the i's dot, the caron is a solid wedge so that it never
# shares the dot's pixel, the macron sits one row higher than the
# diaeresis (so over capitals it takes two rows and an Ā is shorter than an
# Ä), the ring is a round arch, and the narrow tilde is the hump and
# trough of the ~ (the full wave needs six columns, which even-width
# letters such as O and N get).
